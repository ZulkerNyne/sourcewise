# Source fidelity and content review

The complete binding SFR-001 through SFR-010 requirements are in PROJECT_PLAN.md.

1. Use only explicitly selected, approved materials. Enforce permissions before/during
   retrieval in the backend and database; prompts alone are insufficient.
2. Never silently expand scope or mix scholarly explanations without attribution.
3. Require evidence or abstain: "I couldn't establish an answer to this question from
   the selected sources." Distinguish insufficient evidence, no relevant retrieval,
   retrieval failures, and verification failures. Retrieval failure is not proof of absence.
4. Cite each substantive religious claim with resolvable stored references. Do not
   fabricate pages, quotations, metadata, or examples.
5. Distinguish exact quotation, faithful paraphrase, interpretation needing review,
   and unsupported assertions. Strict mode excludes unsupported additions.
6. Preserve original Arabic and distinguish published translations from AI translations.
   Prefer approved human translations for ambiguity; flag uncertainty.
7. Independently check citations, authorization, passage consistency, claim support,
   and cross-source contamination. Semantic checks are probabilistic, not guarantees.
8. Qualified reviewers assess ambiguous or high-risk interpretations. No personalized
   fatwas or unreviewed generated authoritative course content.

## Intake gate (not automated in Phase 0)

Record work, edition, author/commentator, language, publisher, available location
metadata, provenance, document version, and rights evidence. Retain the original
separately from processed text. Verify license or permission for the intended use,
including redistribution of translations and commentaries. A rights_basis field
records the evidence description; it does not itself grant permission.
An authorized reviewer checks rights, text extraction, Arabic fidelity, translation
provenance, and metadata before approving an edition. Preserve a review audit trail.
Corrections require new document versions and retention of historical citations.
No actual religious source has been imported, licensed, or approved in Phase 0.
